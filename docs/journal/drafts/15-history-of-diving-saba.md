# The History of Diving in Saba

Diving in Saba developed from a small, adventurous island activity into one of the defining parts of the island's tourism identity. The broad milestones are clear: dive tourism began developing in the early 1980s, Sea Saba began operating in 1985, and the Saba Marine Park was established in 1987. The details of how individual pioneers explored, named, and taught the island's sites deserve careful firsthand review before publication.

This draft therefore stays deliberately conservative. It is a foundation for owner and staff review, not a finished institutional history.

## A difficult island shaped by the sea

Saba never had the easy coastal access of a broad beach island. Its steep volcanic coastline, limited flat land, and lack of a large natural harbor shaped every part of local life. Before roads and modern harbor infrastructure, people and goods moved through demanding routes such as The Ladder.

That same steep geography later helped protect the underwater environment. Coastal development remained limited, and shore diving did not become the normal model. Boat diving with licensed local operators became central to how visitors experience the marine park.

## Early dive tourism

Official Saba tourism history describes dive tourism as beginning in the early 1980s. Those first years introduced divers to volcanic pinnacles, walls, reefs, marine life, and visibility that were strikingly different from many better-known Caribbean destinations.

The precise stories of who first explored each site, how names were chosen, and how early boats operated should come from people who were present. Otto de Vries is especially important to that review: he began diving in 1978, became Sea Saba's first employee in 1985, and became Saba's first local dive instructor in 1988.

## Sea Saba begins in 1985

Sea Saba has operated continuously since 1985. That makes it a direct witness to most of the island's modern diving history: changes in boats, equipment, training, conservation practice, guest expectations, and the growth of the marine park's reputation.

The current operation should not invent anecdotes about those years. Even simple details, such as early boat names, founding partners, first compressors, or the first time a particular site was dived, need confirmation from people with firsthand knowledge.

## The Marine Park changes the future

The Saba National Marine Park was established in 1987 to preserve and manage Saba's marine resources. It was not created to repair an already damaged environment. Its purpose was to protect the quality of an extraordinary resource before that quality was lost.

The park now circles the island from the high-water mark to a depth of 60 meters or 200 feet, including the seabed and overlying waters. Zoning, permanent moorings, visitor fees, regulation, and diver education became part of the diving experience.

This conservation framework is one reason Saba's diving identity is inseparable from protection rather than merely access.

## From pioneering activity to mature destination

Over time, Saba became known for pinnacles, sharks, turtles, healthy sponges, technical terrain, and a quieter alternative to mass tourism. The island's diving business helped support the local economy while remaining comparatively small and personal.

Sea Saba's own story spans changes in ownership, boats, staff, training agencies, equipment, and guest expectations. Those details belong in the article only after owner review. Otto's continuity, Aaron Smith's long local experience since joining in 2011, and the recollections of other long-serving staff and residents can turn a skeletal chronology into a meaningful history.

## What we should not invent

A credible history article must not invent:

- Colorful founding anecdotes
- Exact site-discovery stories
- Early pricing or equipment details
- Quotes from former owners or staff
- Dates for boat acquisitions or renovations
- Claims about being first without documentation
- Hurricane, accident, or rescue stories
- The origin of every dive-site name

Those details may exist, but they must be collected rather than imagined.

## The simple answer

The verified outline is already compelling: dive tourism emerged in the early 1980s, Sea Saba began in 1985, the Marine Park followed in 1987, and the island developed a reputation for protected volcanic diving unlike anywhere else in the Caribbean. The human detail should come from the people who lived it.

Readers interested in that continuity can learn more about [Otto de Vries](/about/otto-de-vries), [Sea Saba](/about), and the [Saba National Marine Park](https://sabapark.org/saba-national-marine-park/).
