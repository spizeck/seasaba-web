# A Day in the Life of a Sea Saba Dive Guide

A Sea Saba dive guide's day is not only the time spent underwater. It includes equipment checks, weather and site decisions, guest briefings, safety preparation, marine-life interpretation, surface logistics, and the quiet work of resetting everything for the next trip.

Because detailed daily routines must come from the people who actually perform them, this draft is intentionally factual and restrained. It is ready for staff review rather than written as an invented personal narrative.

## Before guests arrive

The working day begins before pickup. Boats, safety equipment, oxygen, first aid, communications, cylinders, rental equipment, and guest requirements all need attention. Weather, sea state, group experience, training needs, and equipment logistics shape the day's plan.

## Welcoming divers

At Fort Bay, guides help guests feel oriented rather than processed. They confirm certifications, experience, equipment, concerns, and goals. Small details matter: a nervous new diver, a photographer with a large camera, a returning guest, or a family with mixed experience each changes how the day is approached.

## The dive itself

Underwater, the guide balances safety, navigation, group awareness, and interpretation. A good guide is watching air consumption, buoyancy, buddy spacing, marine-life behavior, and changing conditions while still helping divers notice what makes the site special.

## Between dives and after the last ascent

Surface intervals involve hydration, tank changes, guest questions, and preparation for the next site. Back at Fort Bay, the visible dive day may end, but equipment care, records, cleaning, storage, and preparation for tomorrow continue.

## Why the work matters

Great guiding is often invisible when done well. Guests remember turtles, sharks, volcanic structure, and the feeling of being looked after. Behind that experience is a chain of preparation and judgment repeated every day.

## The simple answer

A dive guide's work is equal parts logistics, safety, teaching, interpretation, and hospitality. This draft should become richer only after Sea Saba staff confirm or add the real details of their day.
