# How We Choose Your Dive Sites Each Morning

Dive-site selection on Saba is a daily judgment, not a fixed script. The crew weighs wind, swell, current, visibility, mooring access, boat traffic, training needs, guest experience, and the realities of a volcanic island exposed to open water. The goal is simple: choose the best safe and enjoyable sites for the people actually aboard that day.

Sometimes that means a famous pinnacle. Sometimes it means a leeward reef, a shallower macro site, or a different order of dives.

## Conditions come first

A site can be excellent on one day and inappropriate the next. Wind direction and strength affect surface conditions. Swell changes entry, exit, and comfort. Current affects workload and navigation. Visibility influences both safety and enjoyment.

The crew also considers the boat itself, the other groups aboard, and the availability of moorings.

## Divers matter as much as weather

The same conditions do not suit every group. Certification level, recent experience, air consumption, buoyancy, anxiety, photography goals, age, training, and private-guide arrangements all matter.

A mixed group may require compromise or separation into appropriate profiles. A diver hoping for a particular site should tell the crew, but the request remains one input rather than a guarantee.

## Geography provides options

Saba has distinct diving areas. Offshore pinnacles offer dramatic advanced diving. Tent Reef provides varied structure and frequent classic profiles. Ladder Bay combines lava formations, sand channels, and marine life. Wells Bay includes signature sites such as Diamond Rock and Man O'War Shoals. Windwardside sites can be exceptional when conditions allow.

That variety lets the crew adapt rather than forcing a marginal dive.

## Why plans can change

A changed site is not a lesser site by definition. Often it is the decision that turns a difficult day into a good one. Local knowledge means knowing not only where the famous dives are, but when not to use them.

## How guests can help

- Be accurate about experience when booking.
- Share goals early, especially photography or training goals.
- Say if you are nervous, prone to seasickness, or worried about current.
- Listen carefully to the briefing and ask questions.
- Treat flexibility as part of the adventure.

## The simple answer

Sea Saba chooses sites by combining current conditions, local knowledge, safety, and the divers aboard. The process is part of what makes Saba diving personal rather than a fixed tourist circuit.
