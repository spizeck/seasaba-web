# Where to Stay on Saba for a Diving Vacation

The best place to stay on Saba depends less on being close to the dive center and more on how you want to spend your evenings. Because Sea Saba provides complimentary transportation for scheduled dive trips, divers can choose among boutique hotels, traditional cottages, and private villas based on atmosphere, kitchen facilities, views, privacy, and budget rather than walking distance to Fort Bay.

Saba has no large resorts. Most accommodation is small, personal, and integrated into the island's villages. That is part of the appeal, but it also means availability is limited and the best choice for one diver may be wrong for another.

## Start with the villages

### Windwardside

Windwardside is the main tourism village and the easiest choice for many first-time visitors. It has the greatest concentration of hotels, restaurants, cafés, shops, the Saba Tourist Bureau, and the Trail Shop near the Mount Scenery trail.

Stay here if you want to walk to dinner, browse small shops, or enjoy a village atmosphere after diving. It is especially convenient for solo travelers and couples who do not want to arrange transportation every evening.

### The Bottom

The Bottom is Saba's capital and home to the Saba University School of Medicine. It has restaurants, bars, grocery stores, and a more local everyday feel. It can suit travelers who want a practical base or prefer a less visitor-oriented village atmosphere.

### Booby Hill, Troy Hill, St. John's, and Zion's Hill

Smaller residential areas offer cottages and villas with privacy, views, gardens, and quieter surroundings. These can be excellent for longer stays, groups, or travelers who plan to cook some meals. Confirm transportation and walking expectations before choosing a property solely for the view. Saba is steep, and "close" on a map does not always mean an easy walk.

## Hotels, cottages, or villas

### Boutique hotels

Hotels are the simplest option for most short dive vacations. They usually provide a more conventional check-in experience, staff assistance, and convenient access to restaurants. Several properties offer pools, kitchenettes, or ocean-view rooms.

Examples featured in Sea Saba's accommodation guide include Juliana's Hotel, Saba Arawak Hotel, El Momo Cottages, Cottage Club, and Scenery Hotel. Each has a different character, so compare amenities rather than choosing by name recognition alone.

### Traditional cottages

Saba's gingerbread-style cottages are part of the island's identity. A cottage can provide more space, a kitchen, outdoor seating, and a stronger sense of place. This is often a good fit for divers staying a week or longer, couples who value privacy, or travelers who like breakfast at home before pickup.

Cottages vary widely. Some are historic and full of character; others are modernized with private pools or extensive views. Read the details carefully and ask about stairs, air conditioning, kitchen equipment, and access.

### Villas and group properties

For families, dive clubs, or groups of friends, a villa can provide shared living space and better value than several hotel rooms. It also makes early mornings easier because everyone is in one place when transportation arrives.

The tradeoff is coordination. Confirm bedroom layout, bathroom count, parking, grocery access, and whether the property is practical for wet equipment and early departures.

## Transportation matters less than you think

Sea Saba's complimentary shuttle covers transportation between your accommodation and the dive center for scheduled dive trips. That means you do not need to stay in Fort Bay or sacrifice a nicer village location just to make the boat.

A rental car is optional. Taxis can handle restaurants, trailheads, and island exploring. Renting a vehicle may still be worthwhile if you want independence, are traveling in a group, or plan frequent non-diving excursions.

## Recommendations by travel style

### Couples

Choose a Windwardside hotel or cottage if you want walkable restaurants and an easy first visit. Choose a cottage or villa with a kitchen and view if the trip is as much about quiet time together as diving.

### Solo travelers

Windwardside is usually the easiest base. Being near restaurants and village life makes evenings simpler and reduces the need for transportation. A small hotel can also provide more built-in human contact than an isolated rental.

### Groups and families

Look first at cottages, villas, and multi-room hotel arrangements. Confirm sleeping layouts and transportation logistics early. If some people dive and others do not, prioritize access to hiking, cafés, and relaxing outdoor space.

### Underwater photographers

Space and power matter. Look for a room or cottage with a workable table or counter, reliable outlets, and enough room to dry or organize camera equipment. Saba uses 120-volt US-style outlets, which simplifies charging for many North American travelers.

## Questions to ask before booking

- Is breakfast included, or is there a kitchen?
- How many stairs or steep sections are involved?
- Is there air conditioning in the bedrooms?
- Is the property easy for early morning pickup?
- Is there space to dry equipment or organize cameras?
- Are restaurants nearby, and which days are they open?
- Is the rate subject to government tax or nature fees?
- Is the property better for quiet privacy or walkable convenience?

## The simple answer

For most first-time divers, Windwardside is the easiest base. For longer stays, privacy, or groups, a cottage or villa may be better. Because diving transportation is included, choose the accommodation that makes the rest of your vacation enjoyable.
