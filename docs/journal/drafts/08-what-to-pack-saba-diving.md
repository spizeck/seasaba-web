# What to Pack for a Saba Diving Vacation

Pack for two overlapping trips: boat diving in warm tropical water and time on a steep, green island where trails, village restaurants, rain showers, and strong sun are all part of the week. You do not need to bring every piece of dive equipment, but you should bring the personal items that affect fit, comfort, documentation, and safety.

The most important items are simple: certification proof, dive computer, logbook or digital dive history, swimwear, sun protection, and clothing suitable for both boats and hiking.

## Dive documents and essentials

Keep these in your hand luggage where airline rules allow:

- Certification card or digital certification proof
- Nitrox certification proof, if applicable
- Logbook or reliable digital dive history
- Dive computer
- Passport and required travel documents
- Booking confirmations and emergency contact information
- Medication in original packaging
- Travel insurance and dive accident insurance details

A dive computer is required for diving with Sea Saba. Rental computers are available, but if you own one, bringing it avoids learning a new interface on vacation.

## Dive equipment

Sea Saba rents BCDs, regulators, wetsuits, masks, fins, and dive computers. Traveling light is realistic. Still, many divers prefer their own:

- Mask that fits well
- Regulator if you are particular about breathing performance
- Dive computer
- Wetsuit appropriate for your cold tolerance
- Compact reef-safe defog solution
- SMB or personal safety accessories if normally part of your kit
- Save-a-dive items you know how to use

Water temperatures in recent operational records generally range from the high 70s to mid 80s Fahrenheit depending on season. Some divers are comfortable in a shorty or thin full suit; others prefer more warmth on repetitive dives. Think about your own cold tolerance rather than packing only by the average.

Do not bring compressed-gas cylinders in airline luggage. Winair accepts scuba equipment only under its checked baggage rules, and any tank must be empty with the valve removed. Most recreational travelers simply use Sea Saba cylinders.

## Clothing

Saba is casual, practical, and steep. Pack lightweight clothing that dries quickly:

- Swimwear, ideally more than one set
- Light shirts and shorts
- A few evening outfits suitable for small restaurants
- Long-sleeved sun shirt or rash guard
- Light rain jacket
- Hat or cap
- Light jacket or layer for breezy boat rides
- Comfortable walking shoes
- Sandals or easy shoes for everyday use

You do not need formal resortwear. Clean, comfortable clothing is appropriate almost everywhere.

## Sun and heat protection

The Caribbean sun is strong, especially on the water. Bring:

- Reef-safe sunscreen
- Sunglasses
- Hat with a secure fit
- Reusable water bottle
- Lip protection
- Lightweight cover-up

Choose shaded seating when available, hydrate throughout the day, and treat sun exposure as part of dive safety rather than a cosmetic concern.

## Hiking equipment

Saba's trails range from short coastal walks to strenuous rainforest routes. Mount Scenery can be muddy and slippery. Pack:

- Sturdy hiking shoes or trail shoes
- Lightweight socks
- Small daypack
- Water bottle or hydration reservoir
- Snacks
- Rain layer
- Hiking poles if you use them

Do not plan a strenuous Mount Scenery hike immediately after diving. Put major high-elevation hiking on a non-diving day.

## Photography and electronics

Saba is exceptional for wide-angle volcanic structure, turtles, sharks, sponges, macro life, and island scenery. If photography matters, bring:

- Camera body and housing or action camera
- Chargers and appropriate batteries
- Memory cards
- O-ring grease and maintenance supplies
- Lens cloths and desiccant
- Backup storage device
- Waterproof or water-resistant bag for boat days

Saba uses 120-volt US-style outlets, which simplifies charging for many North American travelers. Visitors from other regions should confirm plug and voltage needs.

## Motion sickness and personal care

Even short boat rides can be uncomfortable in the wrong conditions. If you are prone to motion sickness, speak with a physician or pharmacist before travel and bring the preparation that works for you.

Also consider:

- Personal first-aid basics
- Insect repellent
- Hand sanitizer
- Prescription medications
- Any seasickness or hydration supplies recommended by your clinician

## What not to overpack

You probably do not need:

- A full closet of formal clothing
- Heavy dive boots unless your fins require them
- Large bulky beach gear
- Multiple redundant wetsuits
- Tanks or weights
- Large quantities of cash beyond practical needs

Saba uses US dollars, and major cards are widely accepted, but keep some cash for small purchases and the $10 departure tax.

## The simple answer

Bring the personal dive items that affect fit and safety, enough lightweight clothing for boats and trails, strong sun protection, and practical hiking shoes. Leave room for the island to be simpler than a resort packing list suggests.

For required diving items, review [Plan Your Trip](/plan-your-trip). For equipment rental and dive-day logistics, see [Diving with Sea Saba](/diving).
