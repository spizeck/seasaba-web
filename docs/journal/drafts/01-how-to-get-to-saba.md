# How to Get to Saba: A Complete Travel Guide

Most travelers reach Saba through St. Maarten, then continue to the island by a short Twin Otter flight or a scheduled fast ferry. The final connection is part of the adventure, but it rewards a little planning: check current schedules before booking, understand your luggage allowance, leave time for international flight delays, and keep cash available for Saba's departure tax.

Saba sits 28 miles southwest of St. Maarten in the Caribbean Netherlands. There are no large resort shuttles or cruise-terminal crowds here. Instead, the journey ends either at Juancho E. Yrausquin Airport, famous for its very short commercial runway, or at Fort Bay Harbor, a short walk from Sea Saba's dive center.

## Step 1: Travel to St. Maarten

Princess Juliana International Airport, airport code SXM, is Saba's practical international gateway. Major airlines connect St. Maarten with North America, Europe, South America, and other Caribbean islands. Because almost all travel to Saba passes through SXM, your onward connection should be part of the plan from the beginning rather than an afterthought.

When choosing flights, consider:

- How much time you have between your international arrival and the last flight or ferry to Saba
- Whether your ticket protects you if the inbound flight is delayed
- Whether you need to clear immigration and collect luggage in St. Maarten
- Whether an overnight in St. Maarten would make the first day less stressful
- How weather or seasonal schedule changes could affect the return journey

For a dedicated dive vacation, many travelers prefer not to build the itinerary around the tightest possible same-day connection. A relaxed buffer is easier than beginning the trip by rearranging diving, accommodation, and transportation.

## Step 2: Choose the Winair flight or the Makana Ferry

### Flying with Winair

Winair operates direct flights between St. Maarten and Saba using the De Havilland DHC-6 Twin Otter. The flight takes roughly 12 to 15 minutes and lands at Juancho E. Yrausquin Airport. The approach is memorable, with steep green hillsides, blue water, and a runway that feels purpose-built for a small island aircraft.

Winair's current schedule should always be checked directly when booking. Saba service is frequent, but exact departure times and aircraft capacity are date-sensitive. The Twin Otter is much smaller than a typical jet, so baggage allowances matter.

Winair's baggage rules depend on the fare purchased. Some fares include more baggage than others, and dive equipment is accepted only within the airline's checked baggage rules. If you bring a scuba cylinder for any reason, it must be empty with the valve removed. Most traveling divers do not bring cylinders at all, but regulators, computers, cameras, and other valuables should be packed carefully and carried according to current airline rules.

### Taking the Makana Ferry

The Makana Ferry connects St. Maarten and Saba by fast catamaran. Depending on the operator's current timetable and the direction of travel, the crossing is commonly about 75 to 90 minutes. The vessel has air-conditioned interior seating and an open sun deck.

The ferry can be a good choice if you prefer a scenic sea crossing, if the ferry timing fits your flights better, or if you are traveling with luggage that is easier to accommodate by sea. It can also be affected by sea conditions, so travelers prone to motion sickness should prepare accordingly.

Makana's published luggage guidance has included one personal item, one carry-on, and one checked bag with the ticket, with additional or overweight bags subject to fees. Weight limits and fare rules can change, so confirm them before travel.

Current schedules and rates are date-sensitive. Use the operator's official pages rather than relying on a screenshot, social post, or older blog article:

- [Makana Ferry official schedules and rates](https://makanaferryservice.com/)
- [Saba C-Transport Makana information](https://www.sabaferry.com/makana)

### Helicopter and charter options

Private air charters and helicopter transfers may be available for travelers who need a more flexible schedule or who are coordinating a group. West Indies Helicopters is listed by the Saba Tourist Bureau as a transfer option from St. Maarten and St. Barths. These services are premium, capacity-limited, and highly dependent on availability, aircraft, weather, and luggage. Treat them as custom arrangements rather than a fallback you can assume will be available at the last minute.

## Passports, immigration, and inter-island travel

Bring a valid passport. Visa requirements for Saba depend on nationality, so travelers should check the official Caribbean Kingdom visa guidance before booking. Do not rely on advice intended only for another island or another nationality.

Even when traveling between nearby islands, keep travel documents accessible. Each island handles arrival and departure procedures, and ferry check-in can require passports or local identification.

Useful official references:

- [Saba Tourism getting here information](https://www.sabatourism.com/getting-here/)
- [Netherlands Worldwide Caribbean visa guidance](https://www.netherlandsworldwide.nl/caribbean-visa/visa-needed-caribbean)

## Arriving at Saba's airport

Juancho E. Yrausquin Airport sits on a small shelf of land at the edge of the island. Arrival is usually simple because the airport is small, but transportation should still be planned. Taxis are available on Saba, and accommodations can often help arrange transportation.

If you are diving with Sea Saba, complimentary pickup and drop-off between your accommodation and the dive center is included for scheduled dive trips. Airport or harbor arrival transfer arrangements are separate operational details, so confirm what is included when planning your stay.

## Arriving at Fort Bay Harbor

Ferry passengers arrive at Fort Bay, the island's working harbor and the departure point for Sea Saba dive trips. Sea Saba's dive center is at the harbor, so it is an easy landmark once you reach the pier area. If you arrive with luggage, arrange transportation to your accommodation first unless you are arriving close to a scheduled activity and have made a plan in advance.

Fort Bay is also where visiting yachts clear in. If you are arriving on your own vessel, use the dedicated [visiting yachts guide](/visiting-yachts), since clearance, mooring, and dive logistics differ from ordinary airline or ferry travel.

## Packing and dive equipment considerations

You do not need to bring a full set of dive equipment to dive comfortably on Saba. Sea Saba has rental BCDs, regulators, wetsuits, masks, fins, and dive computers. A dive computer is required on every dive, and rental computers are available.

That said, many divers prefer to bring personal items that affect comfort and fit:

- Mask
- Dive computer
- Regulator, if you are particular about your setup
- Wetsuit appropriate for your comfort
- Compact save-a-dive items you know how to use
- Camera equipment, batteries, chargers, and memory cards

Check airline rules before assuming a large camera housing or heavy equipment case will fit within your checked allowance. Winair's small aircraft make weight and size more important than they may be on larger international aircraft.

For clothing and non-diving essentials, see the companion draft [What to Pack for a Saba Diving Vacation](/journal/what-to-pack-for-a-saba-diving-vacation).

## Planning connections and possible disruptions

Small-island travel is usually straightforward, but it is never wise to treat every connection as guaranteed. Wind, sea conditions, aircraft scheduling, inbound international delays, and ferry operations can all affect the final leg.

A practical approach:

1. Book the final leg to Saba only after checking current operator schedules.
2. Avoid very tight same-day connections when possible.
3. Carry medication, documents, swimwear, a change of clothes, and essential dive or camera items in your allowed hand luggage.
4. Keep travel insurance that covers delays, missed connections, medical care, and dive accidents.
5. Save Sea Saba's contact details and message promptly if your arrival time changes.

If sea conditions require a dive trip to change, Sea Saba will work with guests to reschedule or refund the affected portion under the published terms. Travel insurance is still strongly recommended because transportation disruption and dive cancellation are not the same thing.

DAN also recommends planning for the practical realities of remote dive travel, including emergency care, evacuation, and insurance. Review [DAN's travel safety guidance](https://dan.org/safety-prevention/return-to-diving-safely/travel-know-how/) before your trip.

## Leaving Saba

Saba charges a $10 airport or harbor departure tax. The Saba Tourist Bureau notes that payment is cash and that there are no ATMs at the airport or harbor. Keep cash available rather than assuming you can pay by card at the last moment.

For your departure day, allow time for transportation across the island, check-in, immigration or port procedures, and the possibility of weather-related schedule changes.

If you are flying after diving, plan conservatively. Divers Alert Network guidance recommends a minimum preflight surface interval of 12 hours after a single no-decompression dive and at least 18 hours after multiple dives or multiple days of diving, with longer intervals after decompression diving. Do not treat Saba's short inter-island flight as risk-free simply because it is brief or low altitude. Review [DAN's flying after diving guidance](https://world.dan.org/health-medicine/health-resource/health-safety-guidelines/guidelines-for-flying-after-diving/) when building your final dive day.

## The simple answer

The easiest route for most visitors is international flight to St. Maarten, then Winair or Makana Ferry to Saba. Fly if you want the shortest transfer and the famous airport approach. Take the ferry if the schedule or luggage situation fits better and you enjoy being on the water. Either way, book the final leg deliberately, travel with documents and cash for departure tax, and leave enough slack in the itinerary for small-island logistics.

When you are ready to plan the diving itself, start with [diving with Sea Saba](/diving), then use [Plan Your Trip](/plan-your-trip) for accommodation, restaurants, seasons, and island logistics.
