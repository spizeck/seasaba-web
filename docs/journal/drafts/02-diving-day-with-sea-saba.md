# What to Expect on a Diving Day With Sea Saba

A typical diving day with Sea Saba is designed to feel organized without feeling rushed. Depending on the trip you book, your day begins with pickup at your accommodation, equipment preparation at Fort Bay Harbor, a detailed briefing, guided boat diving inside the Saba Marine Park, and time back at the dive center before returning to your hotel or cottage.

The exact schedule depends on whether you book the Advanced 2-Tank Dive, Classic 2-Tank Dive, Afternoon 1-Tank Dive, a course, snorkeling, or a private arrangement. The structure below reflects the shared rhythm of a normal boat-diving day.

## Before your first dive day

Bring proof of certification, your logbook or digital dive history, and a dive computer. If you are Nitrox certified, bring proof of that certification too. Complimentary 32% Nitrox is included for certified Nitrox divers.

A dive computer is required on every dive. If you do not travel with one, rental computers are available. Rental BCDs, regulators, wetsuits, masks, and fins are also available, so you do not need to bring a full equipment bag unless you prefer your own gear.

If it has been more than a year since your last dive, mention it when booking. Sea Saba generally recommends a refresher after a year out of the water and may require one after roughly three years, but the final recommendation depends on your experience, comfort, recent history, and planned dives. Sometimes a private guide is the better answer.

## Hotel pickup and transportation

You do not need a rental car to dive with Sea Saba. Complimentary transportation between your accommodation and the dive center is included for scheduled dive trips.

Pickup times are the times the taxi route begins, not a guaranteed minute when the vehicle will reach your door. Be ready at the listed time and expect normal variation as other guests are collected.

Current published pickup start times are:

## Arriving at Fort Bay

Every Sea Saba dive trip departs from and returns to Fort Bay Harbor. At the dive center, the crew confirms your booking, certification, equipment needs, exposure protection, tank preference, and Nitrox eligibility where applicable.

If it is your first day, allow a little time for paperwork and equipment fitting. If you bring your own equipment, the crew can help you assemble it and understand how the boat is organized. If you rent equipment, you will be fitted before departure rather than asked to solve sizing problems on the water.

Fort Bay is a working harbor, not a large resort marina. The atmosphere is practical and personal. You will meet the captain, guide, other divers, and sometimes snorkelers or course students joining the same boat.

## Equipment preparation

The crew assists with equipment setup, tank changes, and gear handling. Sea Saba cylinders use PRO valves compatible with both DIN and yoke regulators, so most traveling regulators can be accommodated.

If you use your own equipment, check it carefully before leaving the harbor. If you rent equipment, use the time before departure to adjust straps, confirm weights, test your regulator, and make sure your computer is present and working. Small details are easier to fix at the dock than at a mooring.

## Boat departure

Sea Saba's custom 38-foot power catamarans are designed for Caribbean dive conditions. Published features include shaded seating, spacious decks, bow lounging areas, marine heads, camera tables, freshwater rinse tanks, freshwater hoses, and large deep ladders for getting back aboard.

Most dive sites are short boat rides from Fort Bay. That means less time commuting and more time diving, but it also means preparation moves quickly. Listen for the crew's instructions about where to sit, where equipment belongs, when to gear up, and how entries and exits will work.

## How dive sites are chosen

Saba's conditions are real ocean conditions. Exposed pinnacles can have current, leeward sites are usually calmer, and the east side of the island is only diveable in suitable weather. The crew chooses sites for the actual wind, swell, current, visibility, boat traffic, and experience level aboard.

This means a planned site can change. That is not a failure of planning. It is part of diving a small volcanic island responsibly. The goal is to give you the best safe dive available that day, not to force a famous site when another location will be better.

## Dive briefings

Before each dive, the guide explains the site and plan. Expect the briefing to cover:

- Maximum depth and expected profile
- Entry and exit procedures
- Mooring or drift-dive arrangement
- Expected current, visibility, and water temperature
- Important topography and navigation reference points
- Marine life often seen at the site
- Hand signals, buddy arrangements, and guide contact
- Safety stop and lost-diver procedures

Ask questions before entering the water. If you are unsure about current, navigation, buoyancy, photography, or your air consumption, say so before the dive.

## Morning two-tank diving

The Advanced and Classic trips are both two-tank experiences, but they serve different divers.

The Advanced 2-Tank Dive starts earlier, dives deeper on the first dive, and requires Nitrox on Dive 1. Eligibility is a combined certification and experience rule: Advanced Open Water plus 20 logged dives, or Open Water plus 50 logged dives.

The Classic 2-Tank Dive suits most certified Open Water divers. It generally uses sites around the 70-foot or 21-meter range at a relaxed pace. Scuba Diver-certified guests can join the Classic or Afternoon trip with no logged-dive minimum, but they require a private guide because Scuba Diver is a supervised-level certification.

The two products overlap in the shared daily schedule. Advanced covers Dives 1 and 2, while Classic covers Dives 2 and 3. In practical terms, partners with different experience levels can sometimes share part of the day while still diving appropriate profiles.

## Surface intervals

Surface intervals are spent aboard the boat. Filtered drinking water and Gatorade are available. Use the time to hydrate, eat a light snack if you brought one, compare notes with the guide, and prepare for the second dive.

This is also a good time to talk about what you enjoyed or what you want help with. Perhaps you want to improve buoyancy, look for macro life, understand the volcanic topography, or gain confidence around deeper water. Small requests are easiest to accommodate when the crew knows early.

## Lunch and midday options

The Advanced trip returns around 1:00 PM, while the Classic trip continues into the afternoon and returns around 3:00 PM. Because the products overlap differently, lunch plans depend on which trip you book and whether you add the afternoon dive.

Saba has small restaurants in Windwardside, The Bottom, and around Fort Bay, but opening days and hours vary. Do not assume every restaurant is open every day. If lunch is important to your schedule, plan it in advance or ask for current local recommendations.

## Optional afternoon diving

The Afternoon 1-Tank Dive departs at 1:00 PM and returns around 3:00 PM. It can be a lighter standalone dive, a way to add a third dive to a qualifying day, or a shared experience with a snorkeling companion. The afternoon snorkel trip shares the boat with the afternoon dive when available.

Space, weather, and operational needs determine whether adding the afternoon dive is possible. Ask early rather than assuming same-day availability.

## Returning to the dive center

Back at Fort Bay, the crew helps divers off the boat and organizes equipment. Cold drinks are available at the dive center, including water, juices, sodas, and Deep Dive Brewing Co. beer after diving.

Take time to log your dives while details are fresh. Record the site, depth, temperature, gas, exposure protection, weights, conditions, and anything you want to remember. The public [Sea Saba Dive Log](/dive-log) is also a useful way to explore recent sites and sightings, although recreational dive-log observations are not scientific surveys.

## Equipment service after your final day

At the end of your final dive day, you can leave your equipment with Sea Saba. The team rinses, stores, and delivers it back to your accommodation, so you do not need to haul wet gear around the island or spend your final evening cleaning equipment.

Confirm timing and delivery details with the crew, especially if you have an early departure.

## What to bring each day

- Certification and Nitrox proof where relevant
- Dive computer
- Swimwear and towel
- Reusable water bottle
- Reef-safe sun protection
- Hat or light jacket for the boat
- Dry bag for small personal items
- Motion sickness preparation if you need it
- Personal dive equipment or camera if you prefer your own

## The simple answer

Expect a structured but personal day: pickup, preparation, short boat ride, detailed briefing, guided diving, surface interval, and an easy return to Fort Bay. The crew handles the logistics so you can focus on diving well and enjoying one of the Caribbean's most unusual marine parks.
