# Four Decades of Diving With Sea Saba

Sea Saba has been operating since 1985. That continuity now spans more than four decades of change in diving equipment, training, boats, marine conservation, travel expectations, and the island itself. A complete institutional history would be valuable, but it should be built from firsthand memory and documentation rather than invented anecdotes.

This draft is therefore a cautious framework for owner review.

## What is already verified

Sea Saba began operating in 1985. Otto de Vries became the company's first employee that year, after beginning his own diving life in 1978. In 1988, he became Saba's first local dive instructor. The Saba National Marine Park was established in 1987, meaning Sea Saba's story and the park's modern conservation story have developed side by side.

Today, the operation runs guided boat diving, training, snorkeling, sunset cruises, technical diving support, and equipment services from Fort Bay Harbor. Staff biographies document a team with long local experience, instructor credentials, captain qualifications, and deep familiarity with the island's sites.

## What has changed

Even without inventing details, some change is certain. Dive equipment has modernized. Training systems and digital recordkeeping have evolved. Boats have become more capable. Guest communication has moved from paper and telephone toward online booking and instant messaging. Marine conservation has become more central to visitor expectations.

The interesting story is not simply that things changed. It is how a small island operation adapted while keeping diving personal, safe, and rooted in local knowledge.

## What remains consistent

The fundamentals endure: Fort Bay as the departure point, dramatic volcanic sites, changing ocean conditions, the need for careful briefings, and the value of guides who know the island intimately.

The marine park remains central. So does the idea that a good dive operation is measured not only by what guests see, but by how carefully they are prepared and looked after.

## What the full story needs

Before publication, the owners and long-serving staff should answer questions such as:

- Who founded Sea Saba and what was the original vision?
- What boats, offices, compressors, and equipment marked each era?
- Which instructors, captains, managers, and partners shaped the business?
- Which storms, renovations, rescues, or milestones mattered?
- How did training agency affiliations and course offerings evolve?
- What ownership transitions should be explained publicly?
- Which photographs, logbooks, newspaper clippings, or records survive?
- Which stories are accurate but private?

## The simple answer

Sea Saba's longevity is itself meaningful: continuous diving operations since 1985, deep continuity through people like Otto, and a shared history with the marine park. The next step is not embellishment. It is careful interview and verification.
